# Assessment and generative-AI policy template

## Purpose

This template supports transparent, learning-centered use of generative-AI tools in an engineering machine-learning course. It should be adapted to institutional policy and the current course syllabus.

## Three activity types

| Type | Permitted tools | Evidence of learning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-free foundation check | No generative AI | Supervised demonstration of core concepts and code reading |
| AI-assisted technical work | AI may assist as explicitly allowed | Reproducible work, AI-use record, validation, and individual explanation |
| AI-audited work | AI use is required | Critical evaluation of an AI output against independent evidence |

## Student responsibilities

- You are responsible for every result, figure, citation, assumption, and claim submitted in your name.
- You must disclose permitted generative-AI use with the supplied record template.
- You must not upload restricted data, student information, confidential research, or credentials to a third-party tool without authorization.
- You must be able to explain and modify submitted code during an individual verification activity.
- AI-generated code, prose, or analysis does not replace physical reasoning, data provenance, or validation.

## Instructor responsibilities

- State the activity type and allowed tools for each assessment.
- Provide equivalent access or an alternative pathway when required for equity or accessibility.
- Use multiple, proportionate evidence sources rather than an AI detector as the sole basis for a judgment.
- Keep instructor-held data and grading material private.

## Recommended evidence

For AI-assisted work, collect: a problem/split plan, baseline, reproducible artifact, validation/failure analysis, AI-use record, and short individual verification. The aim is to evaluate engineering judgment and capability, not the volume of generated code.
